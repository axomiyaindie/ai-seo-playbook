# 02 — Keywords and Search Intent

> **Part A · Traditional SEO** · [← Back to Traditional SEO](README.md) · Prev: [01 — How Search Works](01-how-search-works.md) · Next: [03 — On-Page SEO](03-on-page-seo.md)

Keywords are where SEO starts — but not in the way most people think. The goal is not to "sprinkle keywords" into text. The goal is to **understand what someone wants when they type a query, and build a page that satisfies that want better than anything else**. That's why this chapter pairs *keywords* with *intent*: the keyword is the surface, the intent is the target.

---

## What a keyword actually is

A **keyword** (or query) is the string a user types into a search box. But behind every query is a person with a need. Modern search engines don't match strings — they infer intent, entities, and context. So when we say "keyword research," we mean **demand research**: finding the queries people use, grouping them by intent, and deciding which ones your site can realistically serve.

### The three dimensions of a keyword

| Dimension | Question it answers | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | *What does the user want?* | Determines the content type you must build |
| **Volume** | *How many people search it?* | Determines potential traffic (and competition) |
| **Difficulty** | *How hard is it to rank?* | Determines whether it's worth targeting *now* |

A keyword with huge volume and brutal difficulty is worse than a modest keyword you can actually win. Always weigh all three.

---

## The four intents (and how to spot them)

Search intent falls into four broad buckets. The same topic can appear in all four — and each needs a different page.

| Intent | User wants | Typical query shape | Content that wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | To learn something | "what is", "how to", "why does", "guide" | Guides, explainers, tutorials, definitions |
| **Navigational** | To reach a specific site/page | brand names, "login", "download" | The actual destination page |
| **Commercial** | To compare before buying | "best", "vs", "review", "alternatives" | Comparisons, reviews, roundups |
| **Transactional** | To do/buy something now | "buy", "price", "coupon", "near me" | Product/pricing pages, signup pages |

### How to identify intent

1. **Read the query literally.** "How to fix a leaky faucet" is informational; "plumber near me" is transactional.
2. **Look at what already ranks.** The SERP is the ground truth for intent. If page 1 is all how-to guides, a product page won't win — no matter how well optimised.
3. **Check SERP features.** Featured snippets, People Also Ask, shopping results, local packs, and AI Overviews each signal intent.
4. **Watch for mixed intent.** Some queries serve two intents (e.g., "running shoes" → both informational and transactional). Serve the dominant one; link to the other.

> **Rule of thumb:** match the *format* of the top-ranking pages before you try to out-rank them on substance. Wrong format = wrong intent = no ranking.

## How to do keyword research (practical workflow)

You don't need expensive tools to start. Here's a workflow that works with free and low-cost sources.

### Step 1 — Seed the list

Start with 5–20 **seed terms** — the core topics your site is about. For an SEO playbook, seeds might be: *technical SEO, keyword research, AI search, llms.txt, structured data*.

### Step 2 — Expand with free sources

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Google autocomplete** | Real queries as you type — instant long-tail ideas |
| **People Also Ask** | Adjacent questions users ask |
| **Related searches** (bottom of SERP) | Semantically close queries |
| **Google Search Console** (if you have a site) | Queries you *already* appear for — highest-value source |
| **Bing Webmaster Tools** | Keyword research tool with volume estimates |
| **Answer the Public / AlsoAsked** | Question-based expansions (limited free tier) |
| **Reddit / forums / Quora** | The language real people use (often differs from tool suggestions) |
| **Competitor pages** | What they target — and gaps they miss |

### Step 3 — Group by intent and topic

Don't build one page per keyword. **Cluster** keywords that share intent into a single page (a "topic cluster"), and link related clusters together. This avoids cannibalisation (two pages competing for the same query) and builds topical authority.

Example cluster for "keyword research":
- *what is keyword research* (informational)
- *how to do keyword research* (informational)
- *keyword research tools* (commercial)
- *keyword research for SEO* (informational)
→ One comprehensive page can serve all four; a separate "tools" comparison page handles the commercial intent.

### Step 4 — Prioritise

Score each cluster on **intent match × volume × winnability**. A simple prioritisation:

1. **High intent match, low difficulty** → do first (quick wins)
2. **High intent match, high difficulty** → long-term plays (build authority first)
3. **Low intent match** → skip, regardless of volume

### Step 5 — Map to pages

For each cluster, decide: *new page, or improve an existing one?* If an existing page already targets the topic, **improve it** rather than create a duplicate. Update the title, headings, and content to match intent, and add internal links from related pages.

## Mapping keywords to the page

Once you've chosen a target, place the keyword deliberately — not repetitively.

| Location | What to do | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Title tag** | Include the primary keyword naturally, front-loaded | Keyword stuffing, clickbait |
| **H1** | One H1, reflects the page's core topic | Multiple H1s, keyword-only H1 |
| **First paragraph** | Mention the topic clearly within the first ~100 words | Forcing the exact phrase awkwardly |
| **H2/H3 headings** | Cover subtopics and related questions | Repeating the same keyword in every heading |
| **Body** | Use natural variants, synonyms, and entities | Exact-match repetition; "keyword density" targets |
| **Meta description** | Summarise the page; include the keyword if natural | Stuffing; it doesn't directly affect ranking |
| **URL** | Short, readable, keyword-relevant | Long parameter strings, dates, stop-word clutter |
| **Internal links** | Link from related pages using descriptive anchor text | "Click here", unrelated links |
| **Images** | Descriptive `alt` text | Keyword-stuffed alt text |

> **Modern reality:** search engines understand synonyms and entities. You do **not** need to repeat the exact keyword a fixed number of times. Write for the reader; cover the topic thoroughly; use the keyword where it's natural.

## Long-tail vs. head terms

| | Head terms | Long-tail |
|---|---|---|
| **Example** | "SEO" | "how to do keyword research for a local plumber" |
| **Volume** | High | Low |
| **Difficulty** | High | Low |
| **Intent** | Often mixed/vague | Usually specific and clear |
| **Conversion** | Lower | Higher |

**Strategy:** start with long-tail (easier to rank, clearer intent, faster feedback), then expand toward head terms as authority grows. A site that only chases head terms usually ranks for nothing; a site that owns many long-tail clusters builds the authority to compete for head terms later.

## Common mistakes

1. **Targeting volume over intent.** A high-volume query with the wrong intent sends traffic that never converts.
2. **One page per keyword.** Creates cannibalisation and thin content. Cluster instead.
3. **Ignoring the SERP.** If you don't look at what ranks, you're guessing at intent.
4. **Keyword stuffing.** Hurts readability and can trigger spam signals; modern engines don't reward density.
5. **Chasing keywords you can't win.** A page that can't crack page 1 earns ~nothing. Prioritise winnable queries.
6. **Forgetting Search Console.** Your own query data is the highest-signal research source you have.
7. **Neglecting AI-search queries.** Conversational, question-shaped queries ("how do I…", "what's the best way to…") are increasingly how people search via AI — structure content to answer them directly.

---

## Key takeaways

- A keyword is a **proxy for intent** — research demand, not strings.
- The four intents — **informational, navigational, commercial, transactional** — each need a different page format.
- **Cluster** related keywords into one strong page; link clusters together.
- Prioritise by **intent match × volume × winnability**; start with long-tail.
- Place keywords **naturally** in title, H1, first paragraph, headings, and body — no density targets.
- **Read the SERP** before you build; it tells you what intent the engine expects.
- Use **Search Console** as your best keyword source; it shows queries you already almost rank for.
